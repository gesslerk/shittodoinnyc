# Who this email is for

Konrad. 34. Lives in Fort Greene, Brooklyn, with his wife and their 1-year-old.
His social life changed completely when the kid arrived. Nothing is casual anymore.
He is not interested in "grab a drink" or texting friends "what are you up to tonight."
He wants to be the friend who already has the plan and asks people to join.
This email exists so that every Monday he has that plan.

Two audiences for the plan:

1. **The guys.** His primary crew. Small groups, one to three friends, occasionally more.
2. **His wife.** Date nights when they get a babysitter. Same taste filter, slightly more polished.

Plus one **family** pick per week for a weekend daytime outing with the 1-year-old.

## Non-negotiables

- **He is not the Time Out NYC audience.** If everyone in the city will be at it because it topped a listicle, skip it. But that is about register, not fame: a name he knows being in town is exactly what he wants to hear about, a trend piece is not. When he rated 483 names in November 2026 he ticked Bill Burr, Chappelle drop-ins, Gervais and Maniscalco, deadmau5, Drake, Linkin Park and Seeed, and left the alt-comedy scene and the deep-cut DJs blank.
- **He is not the Nudge app audience.** He tried it and found it built for 23-year-old women. No brunch parties, no pilates socials, no matcha pop-ups, no "girls night" energy, no run-club-as-dating.
- **No tourist-core Manhattan.** No Times Square, no Midtown rooftop bars, no Hudson Yards, nothing on a bus tour.
- **No influencer spots.** No viral TikTok restaurants, no reservation-bot places. A line is fine when the thing is a classic (he ticked Lucali and Peter Luger); a line for a trend is not.
- **No bottle-service or bro-nightlife energy.** No Meatpacking clubs, no table minimums, no Dumbo rooftop lounges.
- **Price is not the filter.** He will pay whatever it costs if the thing is special. Flag genuine splurges, but never cut a great pick for being expensive.
- **Never invent an event.** Every pick needs a real, current source URL. If a date or price could not be verified, say so plainly in the heads-up line.

## When he can go out

- **Thursday nights.** The main weeknight slot.
- **Weekend daytime.** Saturday and Sunday, morning through late afternoon.
- **Weekend evenings.** Friday and Saturday nights.
- Late nights are fine occasionally. He is a dad, so a set that peaks at 2am is a sometimes thing, not a weekly thing.
- Monday through Wednesday evenings only for something exceptional, and then only in the book-now radar.

## Where he goes

- Home is **Fort Greene**. BAM and Fort Greene Park are his front yard; Barclays Center is a walk.
- Neighborhoods he already loves hanging out in: **Bed-Stuy, Bushwick, Prospect Heights, Clinton Hill, Carroll Gardens, Red Hook**.
- Williamsburg is fine for the right thing (he trains there). Manhattan is fine for the right thing (a great cocktail bar, a fight at the Garden, a talk, a show). Distance is not a dealbreaker; corniness is.

## The ideal pick

All four of these describe the sweet spot. A pick that hits two or more is a strong candidate:

- **Small room, under 200 people.** You can see the performer's face and talk to the bartender.
- **Feels like a secret you had to know about.** Word-of-mouth, community-run, no marketing budget.
- **Big spectacle once in a while.** A fight at MSG or a massive DJ show is worth it a few times a year.
- **Interactive.** He participates: throws punches, sings, plunges, cooks, races, votes. Not just watching.
- **A name in a room too small for them.** Someone he would recognize, playing a room he cannot believe they are in: Fred Armisen at the Bell House, a Tier 1 DJ in a 150-person afternoon slot, an author he reads in a bookstore back room, a chef he knows cooking one night in someone else's kitchen. This is the pick he most wants, and the one the email kept missing.

## What he actually likes, by category

### Music and nightlife

Full breakdown in `music.md`. The short version:

- **Melodic and progressive house is the core.** deadmau5 above all, then Kx5, Eric Prydz, Paul Kalkbrenner, Rebūke, Tinlicker, Marsh, Fred again.., John Summit, Disclosure. Daft Punk is a touchstone. The Kx5 "We Belong Here" show was one of the best nights he's had in the city.
- **Afrobeats, dancehall and amapiano** are his current phase: Burna Boy, Wizkid, Tems, Fireboy DML, Rema, Tyla, Omah Lay, Sean Paul, Popcaan, Shenseea. Brooklyn's Caribbean and West African party scene is a gold mine for him.
- **Hip-hop** runs through everything: Drake, Travis Scott, Don Toliver, Gunna, Lil Baby, Young Thug, Juice WRLD, Pop Smoke, Fivio Foreign, plus a 2000s New York streak (Dipset, Styles P, Lloyd Banks, Big Pun).
- **Berlin techno and German music.** Paul Kalkbrenner, Seeed, Deichkind, K.I.Z, Fettes Brot, Ski Aggu. Berlin-flavored nights and German artists touring NYC are a yes.
- **Latin and reggaeton** (Bad Bunny, J Balvin, Daddy Yankee), **2000s pop-punk and nu-metal nostalgia** (Linkin Park, blink-182, Paramore), **dad rock** (Beatles, Fleetwood Mac, Cream, CCR), and **country** (Luke Combs, Florida Georgia Line) are all real and all fair game for the right night. An emo night or an ABBA night with friends absolutely counts: he dressed up and went to an ABBA-themed dance night in the West Village and loved it.
- Formats he has loved: a day dance party right by the BQE with friends, big warehouse shows, themed dance nights, drag shows with the guys.
- Big room shows a few times a year. Otherwise: small rooms, good sound, DJs who actually play.

### Fights and sports

- **Fight cards: only the huge ones.** A UFC numbered card at the Garden or a championship boxing night at Barclays, a few times a year, in the radar when tickets go on sale. He cut the weekly fights hunt in November 2026 because it kept padding the radar with distant cards and TV nights. A real Muay Thai or kickboxing card in the city is still interesting; a Vegas card on a bar screen is not.
- He **trains Muay Thai at Kings Combat in Williamsburg** and **teaches Krav Maga at KMI Institute in Tribeca**. The combat sports community is his community. Fighter meet-ups, gym showcases, and notable local shows are relevant when they are genuinely good; routine amateur smokers are not.
- **Knicks.** Watching the Knicks playoff run in bars with the guys was a highlight. Playoff games, big rivalry games, and proper watch parties count. Regular-season game tickets are not a priority.
- Fight-night watch parties at bars only when the card is genuinely huge.

### Food and bars

- **Chef collaborations, pop-ups and guest takeovers.** One-night-only menus, visiting chefs, restaurant-in-restaurant nights.
- **Big shared-table meals for a group.** BBQ, Korean BBQ, hot pot, banquet-style Chinese, whole-animal dinners, a tasting menu for six, a boys' night at a steakhouse (Keens was a great one).
- **Bars doing weird programming.** Drag show wrestling, live pro wrestling, bingo, punk trivia, karaoke leagues, oddball variety shows, costume nights.
- **Drink-focused events.** Natural wine fairs, brewery releases, cocktail bar takeovers, mezcal or whiskey nights, seasonal pop-up bars (he loved the Hanukkah-themed cocktail bar).

Places he already loves (do not recommend these as discoveries; do recommend specific events at them, and use them to calibrate taste):

### Culture, comedy and performance

- **Company XIV in Bushwick** is a favorite. Their Halloween burlesque show was a highlight. Anything they stage is a strong candidate, especially for a date night.
- **Small-room stand-up** and **secret shows**: he once went to a random apartment comedy show in Midtown and loved it. Union Hall, Littlefield, the Bell House, Brooklyn Comedy Collective, bar back rooms.
- **His front yard, under-used.** BAM, BRIC, Theatre for a New Audience, Irondale, Mark Morris and Greenlight Bookstore are all within ten minutes of his door. A name in conversation at Greenlight or a strange night at BRIC is a walk home, and the email has barely sent him to any of them.
- **Weird variety and immersive**: burlesque, magic, live wrestling, immersive theater, oddball cabaret, costume screenings. He dressed up for Rocky Horror in Flatbush.
- **Big spectacle culture** for date nights: the Nutcracker at Lincoln Center was one of the best things he has done in the city. New York City Ballet, ABT, a major concert hall night, the Brooklyn Botanic Garden light show. A few times a year, and worth booking early.
- **Fame is not the problem; the arena is.** A big-name comedian on the arena tour is a skip. The same comedian working out a new hour in a 200-seat room is the lead pick of the week. Generic theater is a skip unless it is exceptional.

### Sauna, water and the body

- **Sauna and cold plunge culture** is a real interest: a whole day at the Russian bathhouse with a friend was a highlight. Bathhouse (Williamsburg and Flatiron), Othership, World Spa, Russian and Turkish Baths, QC NY on Governors Island, Brooklyn banyas, contrast-therapy socials, sauna events with DJs.
- **Pools and beaches**: a day pass at the TWA Hotel pool watching planes take off; a beautiful day at Jacob Riis. Rooftop pools, hotel day passes, ferries to a beach.

### Outdoors and active

- **Biking**: rode from Lower Manhattan to the George Washington Bridge with friends. Long group rides, organized rides, waterfront routes.
- **Running and pickup sports**, bouldering, open gym.
- Sunny-day hangs that are actually somewhere: a brewery yard, a pier, a park event with a reason to be there.
- Not interested in day trips out of the city for this email. Keep it in the five boroughs (a Rockaway beach day counts).

### Scenes and interests beyond nightlife

- **Car and motorcycle culture.** He mentioned Daikoku NYC (JDM and car-meet culture) as the kind of deep scene he wants more of. Car meets, Japanese car culture nights, vintage rallies, motorcycle events, cars-and-coffee that isn't corny.
- **Combat sports community** (see above).
- **Jewish culture.** Jewish cultural events, food, music (klezmer in a small room is perfect), museums, holiday-themed pop-ups, talks.
- **German culture and Berlin.** He loves Berlin. German film, German music, German-community events, Oktoberfest that isn't a frat party, Christmas markets, lantern walks for the kid, Deutsches Haus and Goethe-Institut programming. (He listens to German kids' songs with the 1-year-old, so German family events are a double win.)
- **International relations, energy, and commodity trading.** Talks, debates and panels with serious speakers on geopolitics, energy markets, oil and gas, LNG, metals, shipping, trade. Council on Foreign Relations style, Columbia's energy center, 92NY, Asia Society, Open to Debate. Only when the speaker is genuinely high caliber.
- **Creative-scene parties.** A friend's photo studio party was a highlight. Art openings that are actually parties, studio events, design and zine fairs.
- **Holiday traditions with friends.** The Fort Greene Park tree lighting, Dyker Heights lights, the Brooklyn Botanic Garden light show, the Nutcracker, Hanukkah pop-up bars. In season, these are automatic.

## What predictable looks like

After eight issues (September to November 2026) he said the email had become predictable: a DJ at Public Records, an obscure Berlin name at Knockdown, a UFC card sitting on the radar for five weeks, a drag show at All Night Skate. Those are still his taste. They are no longer discoveries. The email earns its keep with the thing he would not have found by reading the Public Records calendar himself, and variety across weeks matters as much as fit within a week. Fred Armisen came through town in a club-sized room and the email did not say so; that is the exact miss never to repeat.

## The family pick

One per week. Weekend daytime. Stroller-friendly or carrier-friendly, done by mid-afternoon, and interesting to the adults too. Examples of the right register: a lantern walk, a German or Jewish community family event, a ferry ride with a destination, a small kids' concert in a real venue, a farmers market with a reason, the botanic garden, a beach morning, a car meet where a toddler can look at engines. Not a generic "go to the playground."

## Voice of the email

Written by a friend who knows the city, not by a marketing team. Direct, a little irreverent, confident. Short sentences. No exclamation-point spam, no "vibes," no "hidden gem," no "must-see," no "immersive experience" as a phrase. Explain why each pick fits *Konrad*, not why it is popular. Every pick includes a copy-paste text he can send to the group chat or to his wife: casual, specific, one or two sentences, sounds like a person.
